### The results of Freese, Ježek, and Nation, one statement each

This is the [Lattices.Free.FJN][] module of the [agda-lattices][] library.

The library proves the results of [Freese, Ježek, and Nation (1995)][]
(FJN) under descriptive names, and a theorem is often spread over several of
them.  This module gathers, for each result of the book that the library
proves, one definition whose type is the statement and one proof of it, and
re-exports them in the book's order.  It proves nothing itself: each statement
and its proof live in the module that proves the result, built from the names
there, and the table below names that module.

The conventions are as follows:

+  **Names**.  A result with a standard name takes it: Theorem 1.11, Whitman's
   solution of the word problem, is `WhitmansTheorem`{.AgdaRecord}, with its
   proof `whitmans-theorem`{.AgdaFunction}.  Every other result is named by its
   number in the book: `FJN-1·19`{.AgdaRecord} is the statement of Theorem 1.19
   and `fjn-1·19`{.AgdaFunction} its proof.  The separator is the middle dot
   `·`, since Agda reserves `.` for qualified names.  The book itself calls
   three results "Whitman's theorem" (1.11, 1.22 and 1.28) and two "Jónsson
   and Kiefer's" (1.21 and 1.30), so only Theorem 1.11 takes a name.
+  **The form of a statement**.  A statement quantifies over the levels of the
   generators and the lattices it speaks of, so it is a type in `Setω`; one
   that quantifies over nothing (1.23, 1.28, 1.29) is a type at level zero.  A
   result in several parts is a record with one field per part, `part-1`,
   `part-2`, and so on, in the order the book gives them: for example
   `FJN-1·4.part-3 fjn-1·4`{.AgdaFunction} is Lemma 1.4 (3).
+  **The reading**.  Each statement says what the book says, in the
   generality of the library: `FL(X)` for any type `X` of generators, a lattice
   generated by `X` as an assignment `X → L` whose extension `FL(X) → L` is
   onto, or every lattice, each with the setoid equality of agda-algebras.
   The library's terms are binary where the book's are n-ary, and its (W) is
   the binary form, equivalent in every lattice.  Decidable equality on the
   generators is assumed only where a computation needs it.  Where a statement
   differs from the book's otherwise, the prose above it in its module says so,
   and the table says so in short.

The other results of Chapter I are not stated here, since the library does
not prove them in full: Theorem 1.1 (Day's doubling), Corollary 1.7,
Theorem 1.10, Corollary 1.14, Lemmas 1.15 and 1.16 (the second is proved for
the `n`-ary terms of the canonical-form modules, not for arbitrary terms),
Theorem 1.22, the last claim of Example 1.24 (its chain has no join; the
strictness of the chain is `ascending-strict`{.AgdaFunction}), Theorems 1.26
(which the book states without proof) and 1.27, and Corollary 1.32.  Nor are
the results of Chapter II, which the library proves only in part where it
proves them (Theorem 2.6 and Lemma 2.7 for a homomorphism from `FL(n)` onto a
lattice with a decided order in [Lattices.Bounded.Preimage][], and the clause
`D(FL(X)) = FL(X)` of Corollary 2.11 in [Lattices.Bounded.Covers][]).

The module is not in the barrel [Lattices.Free][], since it imports
[Lattices.Prime][], [Lattices.Nation][], [Lattices.Generation][] and
[Lattices.Breadth][], which the barrel does not.

  using ( FJN-1·12 ; fjn-1·12 )
